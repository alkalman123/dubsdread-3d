About Rackhouse

Printed by hand, one bed at a time.

Rackhouse is a small-batch 3D-printing studio in Chicago, making gear-storage pieces for climbers — starting with the one we wished existed.

How it started

A desk that needed a fingerboard.

Off-the-wall finger training meant either a full hangboard bolted to a door frame or nothing at all. So the first shape off the plate was small on purpose: a block that sits on a desk with two ports sized to hook fingers into — the Rock Ring, V1.

The lineup grew from there. The Gatekeeper turned the same idea sideways — an oversized organizer that hangs a full trad rack and a helmet off one point — and the Cup Cradle solved the very specific problem of a Nalgene sliding around in a car cupholder on the drive out.

Small on purpose

No warehouse, no pallets of stock. Every order is a print job, queued and run to order — which means colors never really go "out of stock."

Designed for real gear

The design started from an actual gear bench, not a mood board. It changes when it needs to — this is V1 for a reason.

Honest about the medium

It's a 3D print, not an injection-molded product — layer lines and all. We'd rather you know that before it arrives than after.
